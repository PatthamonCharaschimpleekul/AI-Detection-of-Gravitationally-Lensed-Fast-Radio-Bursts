## Verdict
**Do not pursue "tail-calibrated NRE" as a method paper.** Standard NRE is self-calibrated in the tail here; what it loses is power, and more training recovers much of that. Keep the findings below as checks inside the science benchmark paper.

## What we did
- Linear-Gaussian pair simulator with exact marginal LR; shared test set of 2.4M pairs, H0 stratified by rho.
- Trained plain, naive, iw and cond: 3 seeds at 520k parameters and 30k steps, plus one finite-pool seed each (4539 templates).
- Capacity runs on plain: 96 width (1.47M parameters) and 60k steps, seed 0.
- Noise-shortcut check on 20 real CHIME bursts from 2 repeaters (94 real pairs).
- Exploratory, not in the plan: power of a negatives-only validity test (`diagnostic_power.py`).

## Results: unlimited simulation, 3 seeds, 30k steps
Mean [min, max]. Recall is at FPR 3.55e-5, the exact LR's FPR at r >= 1e3. Claim vs observed is log10 ratio for r_hat in [1e3, 1e4), seed 0.

| Method | Recall | Tail RMSE (dex) | E_H0[r_hat] | Claim vs observed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| exact | 0.0877 | 0 | 0.991 | 3.38 vs 3.32 |
| plain | 0.0552 [0.0544, 0.0563] | 0.70 [0.65, 0.73] | 0.997 | 3.31 vs 3.31 |
| naive | 0.0621 [0.0611, 0.0633] | 1.54 [1.53, 1.57] | 0.203 | see caveat |
| iw | 0.0580 [0.0567, 0.0593] | 0.64 [0.61, 0.67] | 0.993 | 3.32 vs 3.28 |
| cond | 0.0610 [0.0605, 0.0615] | 0.57 [0.56, 0.58] | 0.988 | 3.35 vs 3.31 |

- **Caveat on naive's tail:** its [1e3, 1e4) bin holds one raw test negative per seed (error +- 0.43 dex), and its FPR of 3.3e-8 is also one pair. Better-sampled bins: [1e1, 1e2) 1.36 vs 2.20, [1e2, 1e3) 2.33 vs 3.36+-0.03, so it under-claims by about 1 dex. Its bias against exact LR, -1.51 dex, is well sampled.
- **Hard negatives help ranking a little:** naive and cond are 10-12% above plain with seed ranges apart. iw is 5% above, nearly touching.
- **The budget matters more:** plain at 60k steps reaches recall 0.0696 (+26% vs the plain mean), and at 96 width 0.0614 (seed 0 only). Excess BCE falls from 0.0185 to 0.0051 and 0.0070.
- **RMSE alone misleads:** temperature-scaled naive has the lowest RMSE, 0.49 dex, yet over-claims: in [1e3, 1e4) it claims 3.40 and observes 3.10-3.16; in [1e4, 1e6) it claims about 4.5 and observes 3.9-4.0.

## Results: longer training (exploratory, seed 0 only)
Added after seeing the 30k results. Exact LR has recall 0.0877.

| Run | Recall at FPR 3.55e-5 | Tail RMSE (dex) | Excess BCE | E_H0[r_hat] | [1e2, 1e3) claim vs observed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| plain 60k | 0.0696 | 0.49 | 0.0051 | 0.993 | 2.40 vs 2.32 |
| plain 120k | 0.0774 | 0.36 | 0.0028 | 0.995 | 2.40 vs 2.32 |
| naive 60k | 0.0777 | 1.28 | 0.0424 | 0.190 | 2.35 vs 3.40 |
| iw 60k | 0.0738 | 0.40 | 0.0038 | 1.000 | 2.40 vs 2.32 |
| cond 60k | 0.0784 | 0.33 | 0.0036 | 0.985 | 2.41 vs 2.33 |

- **Plain keeps converging:** each doubling of steps lowers RMSE (0.70, 0.49, 0.36) and raises recall (88% of exact at 120k), while staying self-calibrated.
- **Hard negatives keep their gain:** at 60k, cond and naive are 12-13% above plain 60k, and iw 6%. Cond at 60k matches plain at 120k, so it looks about twice as sample-efficient.
- **Cond reaches the 30% size at 60k:** RMSE is 32% below plain and 74% below naive. This does not pass G3: it is one seed, chosen after seeing results, so the seed-range condition is untested. Iw is 18% below plain.
- **Naive's bias does not train away:** it still under-claims by about 1 dex, with E_H0 0.19.
- **Better training does not fix a wrong prior:** under a uniform rho prior, unchanged plain's bias rises from +0.27 dex at 30k to +0.90 at 120k. FPR at r_hat >= 1e3 is 3.34e-4, about 12x its nominal value.

## Results: wrong repeat population
The repeat similarity prior is unknown for real data, so this may matter more than anything above.
- **Size of the effect (seed 0):** plain's FPR at r_hat >= 1e3 rises from 1.65e-5 to 1.99e-4 (12x) if the true rho prior is uniform; the LR with the right prior gives 3.81e-6 there. E[r_hat] becomes about 6.
- **Re-marginalising works when rho is known:** cond gives E_H0 0.98-0.99, similar in bias to a model trained for that prior. Real repeat pairs have no observed rho, so this is a sensitivity analysis, not a correction.
- **Negatives-only test:** E_H0[r] <= 1 holds for a true LR under any population, so a betting e-process on known repeat pairs can reject an anti-conservative score with no lensed examples. 400 trials, level 0.05, plain seed 0:

| Population (E[r_hat]) | n=83 | n=300 | n=1000 | n=5000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominal (1.00) | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| 10% uniform (1.51) | 0.01 | 0.02 | 0.09 | 0.72 |
| 25% uniform (2.27) | 0.08 | 0.31 | 0.86 | 1.00 |
| 50% uniform (3.55) | 0.32 | 0.91 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 100% uniform (6.11) | 0.89 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

Limits: n counts independent pairs, and bursts from one source are not independent, so 83 repeaters give about n=83 unless within-source dependence is modelled. The test is one-sided: it does not flag naive, which under-claims. It uses Gaussian simulator scores, not real waterfalls. The identity is classical and related balance conditions already appear in balanced NRE, so it is a pipeline check, not a new method.

## Results: finite template pool (one seed only)
Recall with 4539 templates: plain 0.0489, naive 0.0609, iw 0.0645, cond 0.0605. E_H0 0.97-0.98 except naive 0.20. Plain drops below its unlimited range; iw lands above its range, which more likely reflects training variance than a real gain.

This is a weak test of data scarcity: templates have about 18 modes at 99% of variance, and noise, amplitude and delay are redrawn every step. It does not show that real data would behave this way.

## Compared with the plan
- **G1 metric flaw (mine):** bias and RMSE conditioned on exact r also measure regression to the mean and power loss. The literal pass should not be read as miscalibration.
- **Plan's rule:** G1 failing meant pivot, G3 failing meant no method contribution yet. By mechanism the thesis fails, so we recommend the pivot.
- **Added after seeing results, so exploratory:** `diagnostic_power.py`, `run_queue2.sh`, the prior re-marginalisation analysis.
- **Unchanged limits:** a 1-D Gaussian benchmark, no time shifts, no time ordering, no source clustering. G4 uses 2 sources only.
